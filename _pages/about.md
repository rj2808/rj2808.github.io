Modern computing faces a fundamental energy bottleneck driven by the limits of charge-based electronics and the rapidly increasing demands of AI. My research aims to address this challenge by developing new computing paradigms based on emergent materials physics. I focus on quantum materials and ferroic oxides, where spin, topology, and polarization provide alternative state variables that can enable low-energy switching, nonvolatile memory, and novel signal transduction mechanisms. By engineering these phenomena at interfaces and in heterostructures, I aim to translate fundamental discoveries into scalable device architectures that complement CMOS and reduce the energy cost of computation.

I am currently a postdoctoral researcher in EECS at UC Berkeley with Sayeef Salahuddin, where I am working at the intersection of spintronic and ferroelectric device platforms for energy-efficient memory and computing. This builds directly on my PhD research at Cornell with Dan Ralph, where I worked on the physical principles at the frontier of spin-based memory and logic.

During my PhD, I co-led the first experimental evidence that altermagnetic symmetry produces a new, exchange-driven mechanism for spin-current generation — published in Nature Electronics and cited over 500 times. I also demonstrated that topological insulators possess the largest spin Nernst response of any known material, and developed van der Waals heterostructure platforms that realize quantum anomalous Hall states at temperatures five times higher than previously achieved.

My work has appeared in Nature Electronics, Nature Materials, Nature Communications, Science Advances, Advanced Materials and has been recognized with the APS Dissertation Award in Magnetism and the Kavli Graduate Fellowship.

## Research interests

- Quantum materials and spintronics
- Topological transport and quantum anomalous Hall systems
- Antiferromagnetic and altermagnetic spin-current generation
- Ferroic devices for memory, neuromorphic, and cryogenic computing
- Scalable materials and device architectures for energy-efficient computing
